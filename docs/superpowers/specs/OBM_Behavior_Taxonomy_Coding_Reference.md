# OBM Behavior Coding Reference — 120 Reliable Entries

## TRUST

**Vulnerability & Psychological Safety**
+ Admits a mistake, error, or gap in their own knowledge unprompted
+ Asks a clarifying question that risks appearing uninformed
+ Explicitly asks a named person for help or additional resources
+ Proposes an unproven or half-formed idea and labels it as such
+ Responds to another's admitted mistake with a question or offer of help rather than criticism
+ Names their own fallibility to invite challenge ("I may well be wrong here — push back on me")
− Attributes negative intent to a teammate without evidence ("he did that deliberately")

**Contractual Trust — Reliability of Character**
+ States a commitment with a specific owner and a specific date
+ Reports back on a previously made commitment without being asked
+ Renegotiates a commitment they cannot meet, before the deadline rather than after
+ Delegates a task together with the authority and resources needed to do it
+ Declines a request explicitly rather than accepting it and letting it lapse

**Communication Trust — Disclosure**
+ Shares information relevant to another's work without being asked for it
+ Gives direct, specific feedback to a person's face rather than about them
+ Receives feedback without defending, and asks a follow-up question about it
+ States explicitly what they do not know or the limits of their information

**Competence Trust — Capability**
+ Asks a specific named person for their expert judgment in their domain
+ Visibly defers to another's expertise on that person's own subject
+ Seeks input from those affected before making a decision that changes their work
− Requests granular status or approval on work that has already been delegated (micromanagement)
− Makes a decision affecting a person's work without consulting or informing them

**Humility & Status Behavior**
+ Attributes a success to a specific teammate by name
+ Corrects a public misattribution of credit in someone else's favor
− Redirects a discussion toward their own accomplishments or standing
− Summarizes team work without naming its contributors

## CONFLICT

**Surfacing Disagreement**
+ States disagreement with a proposal in the same meeting where the proposal is made
+ Names an unspoken concern the group has been circling ("nobody's saying it, but...")
+ Asks another person directly for their dissenting view ("what's the case against this?")
+ Explicitly invites disagreement or assigns someone to argue the opposing case
+ Voices a dissenting opinion against the apparent consensus
− Changes the subject or defers to a later date when a contentious topic surfaces

**Quality of Debate**
+ States observable facts before stating a conclusion drawn from them
+ Explicitly separates observation from interpretation ("the story I'm telling myself is...")
+ Asks the other party for their reasoning or how they got to their position
+ Offers a concrete alternative proposal in response to an objection
+ Supplies factual information or orientation to settle a factual dispute
− Attacks the person rather than the proposal, including labeling ("that's a typical finance answer")
− Attributes a motive to the other party in order to discredit their argument

**Tension Management & Repair**
+ Expresses solidarity or support for a person while disagreeing with their position
+ Names rising tension explicitly and proposes a process remedy ("let's take five and come back")
+ Apologizes for a specific act, naming the act

**Destructive Conflict — Aggression**
− Interrupts or talks over a speaker before they have finished
− Reprimands or criticizes a person in front of the group
− Makes a demeaning or nasty remark directed at a person
− Sends a demeaning or hostile written message (email, chat) about or to a colleague

**Destructive Conflict — Withdrawal & Incivility**
− Stops contributing to a discussion after being challenged

**Resolution & Closure**
+ Summarizes the points of disagreement and states what remains unresolved
+ Proposes an explicit decision rule for resolving the dispute (who decides, by when)
− Allows a live disagreement to go unnamed at the close of the meeting

## COMMITMENT

**Clarity of Mission, Goals & Roles**
+ States the team's objective or deliverable in explicit terms
+ Asks what the priority order is when two goals conflict
+ Names who owns a specific piece of work
+ Restates a decision at the close of a discussion to confirm shared understanding
+ Identifies the resources and constraints available for the task
+ Asks for a decision to be stated explicitly when it has only been implied
− Closes a meeting with no explicit statement of what was decided

**Strategy & Planning**
+ Proposes a concrete plan or sequence of steps for achieving a stated goal
+ Proposes a contingency ("if the vendor slips, we fall back to X")
+ Assigns a named next step with an owner and a date
− Discusses a goal at length without any statement of how it will be achieved

**Buy-In & Decision Closure**
+ States support for a decision they argued against ("I still disagree, and I'm in")
+ Asks each participant explicitly whether they can commit to the decision
+ Sets a deadline for a decision to prevent drift toward consensus-seeking
+ Commits to a decision made under uncertainty, naming it as reversible if wrong
− Proposes an action ("we should do X") that no one takes ownership of

**Shared Vision & Meaning**
+ Describes a specific picture of a desired future state
+ Connects a request to a stated value or interest of the person being asked
+ States why the work matters to them personally
+ States the organizational or customer consequence of the team's work
− Assigns work with no stated rationale or connection to a larger purpose

**Discretionary Engagement**
+ Raises a problem that sits outside their own area of responsibility
+ Asks for more responsibility or scope

## ACCOUNTABILITY

**Peer-to-Peer Accountability**
+ Names a specific missed commitment directly to the person responsible
+ Raises a peer's behavior that is hurting the team, to that peer
+ Asks a peer for a revised commitment after a miss
+ Verbally backs a colleague who has just raised an accountability issue
+ Asks the team to hold them personally to a standard they have just set

**Expectation Setting (Antecedents)**
+ States the standard or criteria for "done" before the work begins
+ Confirms the other person's understanding of an expectation rather than assuming it
+ States the consequence attached to meeting or missing a standard, in advance
− Assigns work without stating what a good outcome would look like

**Performance Monitoring**
+ Examines the actual work product rather than accepting a status summary
+ Asks specific, evidence-seeking follow-up questions about a status claim
+ Tracks progress against a named milestone in the meeting

**Consequences & Feedback Delivery**
+ Delivers specific positive feedback promptly after the performance it refers to
+ Delivers corrective feedback tied to a specific observed act
+ Explicitly links a consequence to a behavior ("because that shipped on time, the client renewed")
− Delivers feedback long after the event, or only at formal review time
− Delivers generalized praise or criticism without naming a specific act

**Ownership vs. Blame-Shifting**
+ States plainly that a miss was theirs, without qualification
+ Separates diagnosis of a failure from attribution of fault
+ Accepts corrective feedback without counter-argument, and states what they will change
− Responds to an accountability challenge with a counter-accusation

## RESULTS

**Collective Goal Focus vs. Individual Status**
+ Subordinates their own function's or unit's interest to a stated team outcome
+ Refers to a shared metric or scoreboard when arguing a position
+ Redirects a discussion from individual contribution back to collective outcome
− Argues a position on the basis of their own status, headcount, budget, or visibility

**Dependability & Delivery**
+ Delivers a committed item on the stated date and reports it
+ Flags a slip early, with a revised date
+ Takes care of shared resources, tools, or documentation without being asked
− Misses a committed date without prior notice

**Coordination & Backup Behavior**
+ Notices a teammate is overloaded and offers or takes specific work from them
+ Helps a new member get oriented
+ States what they need from whom and by when, to synchronize a handoff
+ Gives advance notice to people whose work will be affected by a change they are making
+ Monitors tools, resources, or external conditions and reports an emerging risk
− Completes their own task while a dependent teammate visibly fails

**Progress Monitoring & Adaptation**
+ Reports actual progress against plan, including bad news
+ Proposes a change of approach when monitoring shows the plan is failing
− Reports status in terms of activity rather than outcome ("we've had a lot of meetings on it")

**Recognition & Collective Motivation**
+ Publicly recognizes a specific individual's contribution to a result
+ Marks a team milestone or accomplishment explicitly
+ Expresses confidence in the team's capability to achieve the goal
+ Names and defuses frustration or stress in the group
− Passes over a completed result with no acknowledgment

**Continuous Improvement & Learning from Failure**
+ Proposes a change to an existing process or way of working
+ Frames a failure as information and states what was learned from it
+ Runs or participates in a retrospective that produces a named, owned change
+ Takes a considered risk on an unproven approach and says so
− Treats a failure solely as a fault to be assigned
